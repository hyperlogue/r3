The experiment

Behind the curves.

A signal with a little extra detail

The ground truth combines a decaying wave with a smaller ripple:

f(x) = e−0.22x sin(1.4x) + 0.12 cos(3x)

Your model has one decaying wave and a constant offset:

ĝ(x) = A e−dx sin(ωx + φ) + b

Start with A = 1, d = 0.22, ω = 1.4, φ = 0, and b = 0. This matches the main wave, but the model has no second cosine term. Even a close fit leaves a residual. The “Close fit” preset is a useful reference, not a numerically optimized solution.

Three ways to measure the miss

For each of 401 equally spaced points from 0 to 10, compute e = ĝ(x) − f(x). All three metrics use those same points and the signal’s units.

Root mean square error
√mean(e²). Squaring gives larger misses more weight.
Mean absolute error
mean(|e|). The average size of a miss, ignoring its sign.
Maximum absolute error
max(|e|). The worst miss among the sampled points; the continuous maximum may fall between samples.

The residual plot shows signed error: positive means the model is too high, negative means it is too low. Its vertical scale adapts to the largest error; the main signal plot keeps a fixed scale for comparison.

Try this

Choose “Phase drift”, then lower the frequency until the peaks line up. Try “Slow decay” and watch what happens near x = 10. Use the point inspector to compare both formulas at a specific x.
